Day 0One sentence

You type a sentence. Envoy works out the rest.

No forms, no categories to hunt through. “Outdoor wedding for 150 guests in fall, near Austin.” That is the entire brief. Envoy reads it the way a planner would — pulling out the constraints you didn't think to state.

Week 1The lineup

Every vendor the project needs — and why it needs them.

This is the part that normally takes a fortnight of evenings. Envoy returns a ranked shortlist by category, and, more usefully, the reason each category is on your list. You cut what you don't want before anything goes out.

Week 2First contact

Envoy writes the introductions. You approve every one.

Same dates, same details, twenty-three times over — the job nobody wants. Envoy drafts each one in your voice, from your address. Tell it the “hope this finds you well” opener feels stiff and it won't use it again.

Draft — introduction to a floristExample
To: Wildflower & Fern Studio From: you@yourdomain.com Subject: Outdoor wedding florals — October 12, 150 guests

Hi Sarah, We're getting married on October 12th just outside Austin — an outdoor ceremony and reception for about 150 guests. We're after garden-style arrangements in a muted autumn palette. Do you have availability that date, and could you share pricing for ceremony florals plus fifteen tables? Thanks so much, Maya & James
