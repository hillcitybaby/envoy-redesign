Each bar is 320 → 1600px. The orange band is where that component overflows the space it has. Because the page clips horizontal overflow, none of this produces a scrollbar — it is silently cut off, which is why it is easy to miss.

One root cause

32 fixed pixel widths are declared outside any media query, and the type scale is almost as rigid: of the desktop's 12 sizes, only 4 are ever restated, and all of those are below 900px. Between 900 and 1440 — the whole laptop and tablet range — nothing in the type scale changes at all. On mobile it is absolute: 20 sizes, none restated at any width, so a 320px phone renders exactly the type intended for 402.

Three things the implementation turned up

  • Releasing a height has to release the minimum too. The breakpoints that said height:auto were there to cancel a fixed height. Once those heights became minimums, height:auto no longer cancelled anything, and the panel captions were clipped at 768–900. Every one of those overrides now sets min-height:0 as well.
  • The hero reflow moved from 900 to 1100. The hero card is absolutely positioned with a percentage height, so it was tight from about 940px up. Rather than keep patching it, it now reflows to the stacked layout at 1100 — a content-driven point, like the other two.
  • Two places were losing content under a reader's text settings rather than growing: the stacked rail clipped a caption, and the composer's text field swallowed overflow. The rail no longer clips in the stacked layout — the overlap it was hiding only exists in the side-by-side one — and the field scrolls instead.

Now enforced

Four more laws run on every build, alongside the five button laws:

  1. No text clipped by an ancestor, at 320, 375, 768, 1024 and 1440.
  2. Nothing extends past the viewport.
  3. The WCAG 1.4.12 text-spacing overrides cost no content, tested at 375 and 1024.
  4. No font-size or structural height is hard-coded outside the system.

That last one is what stops the scales eroding: a font-size:15px or a new height:380px anywhere in either build now fails the build by name.

What this cost

The page no longer matches the Figma frames to the pixel — spacing moved by 1–4px in 42 places, the type scale is fluid rather than fixed, and the hero reflows 200px earlier. That was the trade you approved, and it is what let every other number above go green.

Checked against the standards

Before implementing, I tested the proposals themselves. Three of them were wrong, and the testing turned up a failure I had not looked for.

Correction 1  The type scale must be in rem, not px

I proposed the scale in pixels. That ignores the reader's own font-size setting. I raised the browser default from 16px to 20px and measured the page:

ElementAt 16px defaultAt 20px defaultResponds?
Hero headline40px40pxno
Hero lede18px18pxno

Nothing moves. Page zoom still works, so this is not a WCAG 1.4.4 failure, but someone who has set a larger default font gets no benefit anywhere on the page. The scale should be in rem, and the fluid steps need rem at both ends too — a px floor inside clamp() locks the minimum no matter what the reader has chosen.

Correction 2  min-height has to apply to all 20 fixed heights, not the two I named

I ran the WCAG 1.4.12 Text Spacing test, which the audit had not covered: a reader may override line height to 1.5, letter spacing to 0.12em, word spacing to 0.16em and paragraph spacing to 2em, and no content may be lost. The page fails this today, in far more places than the two I proposed to fix:

BlockNeedsHasAt

There are 20 fixed heights across the two builds, nearly all measured straight off the Figma frames. Every one of them should be min-height. That keeps the designed proportion at the design widths — the content already fits exactly there — while letting the box grow when a reader's settings or a narrower column demand it. Only genuinely fixed graphics stay fixed: the 1px rule, the icon boxes, the carousel dots.

Correction 3  The composer button should stack, not become an icon

I suggested dropping to an icon-only arrow below 360px. That trades a clear label for a glyph at exactly the moment the screen is hardest to read, and it is the kind of thing that tests badly. The standard pattern is better: below about 400px, let the button go full-width underneath the field. The label survives, and the target becomes a comfortable 44px tall across the whole width.

New finding  One paragraph runs to 107 characters a line

Comfortable measure is roughly 45–75 characters. At 1024px the business paragraph goes full width when that section stacks, giving 107 characters per line. Body copy in the stacked layouts needs a max-width of about 65ch. Everything else sits inside the range at every width I tested, apart from a few deliberately short lines in narrow columns.

What held up

  • Reflow (WCAG 1.4.10). At 320px there is no horizontal scrolling and nothing is lost — already true, and the fixes keep it that way.
  • Content-driven breakpoints. Choosing 1420 and 1460 because that is where the content stops fitting is the right method; round numbers chosen in advance are the thing to avoid.
  • The 4px spacing grid and the 9-step scale.
  • Target sizes — verified at 24px minimum in the last pass, unaffected by any of this.
  • min-height over height — right instinct, just far too narrowly applied.

Recommended fixes

Split into the one-line changes that remove the breakage now, and the deeper change that stops it recurring.

Small  Five changes that close all four broken ranges

Both height → min-height swaps are safe: at the design widths the content already fits exactly, so nothing moves at 1440 or 402 — they only stop clipping when it doesn't. The two stacking points simply stack earlier, using a layout that already exists.

Below about 400px the composer button goes full-width under the field, keeping its label and gaining a 44px target. Body copy in the stacked layouts gets max-width:65ch.

Larger  Make the two rails fluid

Stacking earlier is a workaround; the rails are rigid because the cards are positioned with hard pixel offsets (left:0 / 217 / 434 / 651) derived from the Figma frame. Making them percentage-based would let both sections stay side-by-side right down to the stacking point and remove two of the fixed widths for good. It is a contained change — the offsets are already computed from CSS variables for the hover interaction — but it will shift the panel overlap by a pixel or two from the Figma match, so it is your call whether that trade is worth it.

Worth being honest about the cost: applying this moves things by 1–4px in a lot of places, and the page will no longer match the Figma frames to the pixel. Everything we have built so far has been measured against those frames, so this is the first change that deliberately breaks that tie. Breakpoints

There are currently five, set independently in two files: the view switch at 768, then 1400, 1180, 900 and 560 inside the desktop build, plus 521 in the mobile build. None of them line up with where things actually break.

NameWidthWhat changes
sm< 600Mobile design, single column, fluid composer
md600 – 1023Mobile design centred up to 520, or the desktop stacked layout above 768
lg1024 – 1439Desktop, sections stacked rather than side-by-side
xl≥ 1440Desktop at full width — the Figma frame

Moving the two stacking points to 1420 and 1460 is the pragmatic version of this: it keeps the existing structure and closes both bands. Making lg a real breakpoint at 1440 is the tidier version, because that is the width the desktop frame was drawn at — below it, the side-by-side layouts were never designed to fit.

Suggested order

  1. The small fixes. All 20 heights to min-height, the two stacking points, the composer. They close every broken range and the 1.4.12 failures, and nothing moves at the design widths.
  2. The type scale. Biggest visible improvement in the tablet range, and it retires the fractional sizes.
  3. The spacing scale. Largest diff, least visible gain — worth doing, but only once you have decided to let go of the pixel-for-pixel Figma match.
  4. Fluid rails. Optional. Removes the last two rigid widths.

What I would add to the laws

Once the fixes are in, the build check can enforce them: no text clipped by an ancestor at any tested width, nothing past the viewport, every font-size and spacing value drawn from the scale, and the WCAG 1.4.12 text-spacing overrides applied as part of the run. That last one is the test that caught the most here, and it is cheap to automate.
